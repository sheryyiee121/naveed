What Is an EPG and How Does It Work in IPTV?

The programme guide explained, including fixes when listings look wrong.

Updated 14 October 2026·6 minute read

The programme guide explained, including fixes when listings look wrong.

EPG means programme guide

An electronic programme guide shows what is on now and next, often several days ahead. It is the grid of channels and times you scroll through to find something to watch.

A good guide is the difference between a service that feels like television and one that feels like a folder of files. It tells you what is on, when it starts, and what is coming next.

Why guides drift

Timezone settings, a stale cache or a failed data refresh can make listings appear late or empty. The channel itself is usually fine; the guide data behind it is what has gone stale.

Guides are downloaded separately from the streams. If the download fails, or the device clock is wrong, the listings can sit an hour out or vanish entirely.

Quick fixes

Confirm the UK timezone, refresh EPG data and restart the player. Avoid repeated full reloads while the first sync is still running, because they can queue up and slow things further.

If only one channel's listings look wrong, try another channel first. A guide problem that affects everything points to settings or data; one that affects a single channel points to that channel's data.

Favourites save time

Build a short favourites list so the guide opens around the channels your household actually watches. Scrolling past hundreds of channels you never open is the fastest way to make a guide feel slow.

Most players let you hide or reorder channels. Ten minutes of tidying pays off every time someone opens the guide.

EPG versus catch-up

The guide also shows which programmes can be replayed, where catch-up is available. A replay icon or a backwards scroll through the guide usually marks catch-up programmes.

Not every channel offers catch-up, and availability depends on the source. The guide shows what is offered; it does not create replays that do not exist.
