IPTV on Sky Glass: What to Know

Why Sky Glass differs from a Firestick and how an external device can help.

Updated 14 October 2026·6 minute read

Why Sky Glass differs from a Firestick and how an external device can help.

Sky Glass is a closed platform

Its built-in app selection is controlled by Sky, so many third-party IPTV players are not directly available. That is the single biggest difference from a Firestick, where you choose your own apps.

It is not a fault with your television. Sky Glass is designed around Sky's own apps and services, and it does not offer the open app store a Firestick does.

Use an external device

A Firestick or another compatible streaming device connected by HDMI is usually the simpler route. The IPTV UK subscription runs on the external device, and Sky Glass simply displays it.

This is the setup most Sky Glass owners use for services outside Sky's app list, and it keeps the Sky Glass interface untouched for everything else.

Select the right input

Connect the device, switch Sky Glass to its HDMI input and complete setup on the external device. Everything from login to channel guide happens on the Firestick side.

If the picture does not appear, check the input menu first. Sky Glass can sit on the wrong input while the Firestick waits patiently on another one.

Keep controls simple

Enable HDMI-CEC if supported so one remote can handle basic power and volume functions. That avoids the two-remote shuffle that puts many people off external devices.

Give the HDMI input a clear name if Sky Glass allows it, so the household knows which input is which.

What to expect

Expect the same channels, guide and on-demand library as on any other compatible device. The only change is where the service runs.

Picture quality depends on the same things as ever: connection, device and television settings. Sky Glass displays a 4K picture well when the connection supports it.
