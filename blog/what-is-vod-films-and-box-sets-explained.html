What Is VOD? IPTV Films and Box Sets Explained

How on-demand libraries differ from live channels and catch-up.

Updated 14 October 2026·6 minute read

How on-demand libraries differ from live channels and catch-up.

Watch on your schedule

Video on demand lets you choose a film or episode rather than waiting for a channel schedule. You press play when you are ready, pause when life interrupts, and come back later.

That freedom is the whole point. Live TV runs to the broadcaster's timetable; on-demand runs to yours. Most households use both, and a good IPTV UK subscription keeps them side by side.

Browse by category

Most players organise VOD by genre, year or recently added titles. Search is faster for a specific programme, and browsing works better when you just want something to watch.

If the library feels endless, start from a genre you enjoy and use favourites for anything you want to find again quickly.

Picture quality varies

The label reflects the source, but your connection, device and television still affect the final picture. A 4K label does not guarantee a 4K picture on a slow connection.

As a guide, allow around 10 Mbps for steady HD and 25 Mbps or more for 4K. A wired connection is the simplest way to keep quality stable.

Use parental controls

Protect mature categories with a PIN and create age-appropriate favourites for younger viewers. A library that mixes family films with grown-up titles needs a gate between them.

Set the PIN before the children find the library, and check that it covers both the film categories and any adult sections the service includes.

VOD, live and catch-up together

Live TV is happening now. Catch-up replays recent programmes inside a window. VOD is a library you browse at leisure. All three can sit in one service, and each suits a different moment.

Knowing which is which stops the common confusion of hunting for a film in the live guide, or expecting a live channel to behave like a library.
